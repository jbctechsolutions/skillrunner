## Critical Unknowns That Could Change the Entire Analysis

These are gaps where new information could fundamentally alter the strategy:

1. **How many consultants actually use CLI-based workflow tools?** The entire SAM calculation rests on an unvalidated 20-30% assumption. If it is 5%, the market is 4x smaller. If it is 50%, it is 2x larger. *This is the single most important unknown.*

2. **Will AI chat platforms (ChatGPT, Claude) add reproducible workflow features?** If OpenAI or Anthropic ships saved, shareable, version-controlled workflow templates in the next 12 months, Skillrunner's core differentiation narrows dramatically.

3. **Does the "fractional CTO" segment actually buy tools, or do they build their own?** Fractional CTOs are technical -- they may prefer to build custom scripts over buying a workflow engine. No data exists on tool purchasing behavior for this segment.

4. **Is the church/non-profit vertical big enough to sustain a business?** The 10K-20K consultant estimate and the church tech budget data are both poorly sourced. The actual serviceable market could be a fraction of what is estimated.

5. **What is the real competitive landscape?** The research found no direct competitor, but stealth startups, internal tools at consulting firms, or pivoting competitors could emerge at any time. The moat is narrow.

6. **Can an open-source CLI tool achieve sufficient conversion rates?** The 0.5-3% benchmark is from established open-source SaaS. A new entrant in a niche market may convert at lower rates, especially if the free tier is fully functional.

7. **Will federal AI legislation preempt state laws?** This could either simplify or complicate the compliance picture. The current patchwork is expensive; preemption could reduce compliance costs significantly.

8. **What are actual churn rates for developer CLI tools?** SMB SaaS benchmarks (3-7%) are used as proxies. If CLI tools have higher churn (because users outgrow them or build their own), unit economics break down.
